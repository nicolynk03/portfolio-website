Honours thesis

My honours project explores the application of machine learning to forecast daily patient arrivals in the emergency department.

Beyond predicting overall volumes, the work extends to sub-categories, including ward allocation and patient complexity, providing more granular insights for hospital resource planning.

I experimented with a range of models, including SARIMAX for time-series forecasting, LSTM for sequence learning, and Random Forest for robust feature-driven predictions. Throughout the project, I gained hands-on experience in feature engineering, data cleaning and pre-processing, training and testing algorithms, alongside comparing algorithm performance to determine the most suitable approaches for different forecasting objectives.

Note: the project is contained in a private repository as it contains sensitive data.

AUnity (GovHack 2025)

My friends and I created AUnity as part of our submission for GovHack 2025 over three days, this website is designed to support newcomers and locals in Australia. It helps users learn about Australian values, history, and daily life while connecting with their community.

The key features of AUnity include built-in language selection, an AI chatbot, and trusted information sources to make knowledge simple and accessible for everyone.

For this project, I contributed primarily to the frontend development, creating a clean, user-friendly interface that encourages exploration and engagement with the platform.

AUnity demonstrates my skills in HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and responsive web design, while showing my ability to design inclusive digital experiences that serve diverse communities.
